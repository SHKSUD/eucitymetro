Is Piazza Navona wheelchair accessible?
Yes, Piazza Navona is a massive, level open space ($5\text{Hz}$ vibration). It is one of the most wheelchair-friendly areas in Rome. There are no steps to enter the square from any of its side streets. The area around the 'Fountain of the Four Rivers' is flat and allows for easy $360\text{-degree}$ movement.
Where are the accessible restrooms at Piazza Navona?
The 'Museum of Rome' (Palazzo Braschi) located at the south end of the square has excellent, modern PRM restrooms accessible via lift. You do not need to visit the museum to use the cafe and restrooms on the ground floor.
What is the best taxi drop-off for Piazza Navona?
The best drop-off is 'Corsia Agonale' ($41.898900, 12.473100$). This is a short side street that opens directly into the center of the piazza. It is level and paved with smooth stone, allowing for a 0Hz taxi-to-piazza transition.
What is the sensory profile of Piazza Navona?
It has a 'vibrant and artistic' sensory profile. Acoustically, it is medium ($65\text{dB}$) with street musicians and splashing fountains. Visually, it is very wide and bright. Physically, it is stable ($5\text{Hz}$). It feels much less cramped than Trevi or the Pantheon.
Can I enter the church (Sant'Agnese in Agone)?
Yes, but the main entrance has steps. There is a side entrance with a ramp—you may need to ask the church attendants to open it for you. Once inside, it is level.
How do I ask for the ramp at the church?
Find the attendant at the door and say: 'Per favore, c'è un'entrata con rampa per sedia a rotelle?'. They will usually guide you to the side door.
