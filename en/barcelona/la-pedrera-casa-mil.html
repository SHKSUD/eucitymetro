Logistics Intelligence

Is La Pedrera wheelchair accessible?
Yes, La Pedrera is highly accessible. The main floor, the reconstructed apartment, and the attic museum are 100% level and connected by high-capacity lifts ($0\text{Hz}$ vibration). The roof terrace, which is famous for its uneven 'rolling' hills, is partially accessible via a dedicated lift that leads to a flat viewing platform. This allows wheelchair users to experience the rooftop atmosphere and see the chimneys without having to navigate the stairs.
Where are the accessible restrooms at La Pedrera?
There are high-quality PRM restrooms on the ground floor and in the attic level. They feature 2.0-meter turning radii, support rails, and are maintained to a very high standard. The museum provides free manual wheelchairs if your power-chair is too wide for the historic lift doors (70cm limit).
What is the best taxi drop-off for La Pedrera?
The best drop-off is at 'Carrer de Provença, 261' ($41.395300, 2.161900$), right at the side of the building. This avoids the heavier crowds of Passeig de Gràcia and offers a flush curb for the taxi ramp.
What is the sensory profile of La Pedrera?
It has an 'organic and sculptural' sensory profile. Acoustically, it is medium ($60\text{dB}$), but the internal courtyards provide a unique vertical soundscape. Visually, the undulating walls and natural light are very stimulating. Physically, it is ultra-smooth ($0\text{Hz}$). For those with sensory sensitivities, the attic is the quietest and most 'cocoon-like' space.
When is the best time for a wheelchair visit?
Daily at 09:00 AM. Being among the first to enter ensures you can use the lifts without waiting and navigate the 'Pedrera Night Experience' (if visiting late) with more space to maneuver on the rooftop platform.
How do I ask for the rooftop lift?
Ask the staff: 'On és l'ascensor per anar al terrat?' (Where is the lift for the roof?). They will assist you as the roof lift requires a staff key and specific guidance to the accessible platform.
