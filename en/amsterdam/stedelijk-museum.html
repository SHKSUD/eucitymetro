Is the Stedelijk Museum accessible?
Yes, it is arguably the most accessible museum in Amsterdam. Designed with a 'no-threshold' philosophy, it features wide galleries, smooth resin floors ($0\text{Hz}$), and large elevators that can accommodate multiple power-chairs.
Where are the accessible restrooms at the Stedelijk?
High-spec PRM restrooms are located on the lower ground floor (Level -1) and near the library. They are modern, featuring automated doors and ample turning space ($2.0\text{m}$).
What is the best taxi drop-off for the Stedelijk?
The best drop-off is 'Museumplein 10' ($52.358100, 4.879700$), directly at the entrance canopy. It is a dedicated 0Hz drop-off zone with no curb.
What is the sensory profile of the Stedelijk?
It has a 'clean and acoustic' sensory profile. The modern wing is very bright and airy. Noise levels are moderate ($60\text{dB}$). Physically, the surfaces are perfectly stable.
Can I see the large-scale installations?
Yes, all contemporary art halls are designed with wide clearances ($>3\text{m}$) specifically to ensure wheelchair users can navigate around large sculptures and installations.
Are there elevators to the historic wing?
Yes, the museum has integrated the 19th-century building with the new wing via a series of large, glass-enclosed lifts that bypass all historic stairs.
