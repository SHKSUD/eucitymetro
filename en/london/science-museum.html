Logistics Intelligence

Is the Science Museum wheelchair accessible?
Yes, the Science Museum is a model for modern accessibility. All galleries across all seven floors are 100% step-free. The flooring is primarily polished concrete ($0\text{Hz}$ vibration). The lifts are massive and very frequent. All the iconic objects, like the Apollo 10 capsule and the steam engines, are viewable from a seated position with no barriers.
Where are the accessible restrooms at the Science Museum?
There are modern PRM restrooms on every floor. The 'Wellcome Wing' (Level 0) features a 'Changing Places' toilet which includes a hoist, a height-adjustable changing bench, and plenty of space for two carers. This is one of the best facilities in the South Kensington area.
What is the best taxi drop-off for the Science Museum?
The best drop-off is 'Exhibition Road' ($51.497500, -0.174600$). Taxis can pull up right in front of the main entrance which is completely flush with the sidewalk. This is a pedestrian-friendly zone with very smooth asphalt ($5\text{Hz}$).
What is the sensory profile of the Science Museum?
It has an 'interactive and kinetic' sensory profile. It can be very loud ($80\text{dB}$) in the main halls due to school groups and machinery. The 'Deep Blue' and 'Who Am I?' galleries are much darker and quieter ($50\text{dB}$) for those needing a sensory break. Physically, it is ultra-smooth ($0\text{Hz}$).
Is the IMAX cinema accessible?
Yes, the IMAX theatre has dedicated wheelchair spaces at the back and front with level access. It is best to book these in advance. The sound is very high-intensity ($90\text{dB}+$), so ear protection is recommended for those with sensory sensitivities.
How do I find the 'Changing Places' toilet?
Go to the 'Wellcome Wing' on the Ground Floor (Level 0) and ask at the info desk: 'Where is the Changing Places facility?'. They will provide you with the access code or key.
